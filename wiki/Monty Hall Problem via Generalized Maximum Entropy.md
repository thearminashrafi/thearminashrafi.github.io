The Generalized Maximum Entropy Method, gives us a recipe to update beliefs, given new information, which is encoded as constraints over the set of new posteriors, a.k.a updated beliefs. 


The Monty Hall Problem, is an interesting probability riddle that highlights the importance of how one constructs the sample space of possible outcomes, and how one views belief updates. 


## The Riddle

Imagine your a contestant at a game show, in which you are presented with 3 closed doors. Behind one of those doors, is a car, and the other two doors are empty (or have a goat behind them). The car is yours, if you can guess which door has the car behind it. You are first asked to pick a door, as your candidate guess.


The Game show presenter, will then give a you hint, by revealing one of the doors where the car isn't (it would be pointless to show you a door with the car behind it!). 

Now, you are asked again, whether you would like to keep the door you had chosen, or switch to the other door.

Should you Switch your candidate door?
