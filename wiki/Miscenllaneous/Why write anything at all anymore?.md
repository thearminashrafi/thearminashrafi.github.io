
Given the advances with Large Language Models, and their capabilities in text generation, important considerations are required in the way we write, to make the process of writing more helpful for us as the writer, and also be transparent with others on how the text was generated. 

LLM generated content is cheap and abundantly produced, and is perhaps even less useful as a static post. It is almost always more useful to use LLMs interactively, where one can ask many follow up questions, reformulate questions, virtually indefinitely without limit. It puts into question, the value in writing anything by yourself. I argue that there is, provided we highlight  and discriminate between a few impactful distinctions. 

Until recently, writing complexity was a [costly signal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Costly_signaling_theory_in_evolutionary_psychology) to determine the predicted quality and usefulness of a piece of writing. As a writer, it was possible to simply produce the writing, and show it to others. The quality and complexity of the writing, "spoke for itself". However, the abundance with which one can produce writing of *apparently* unlimited complexity using Large Language Models, means that this is no longer a viable signal to rely on. 

Costly Signals are not only for others, they can also be signals we send to ourselves. One could use the *apparent* complexity, consistency and structure of the writing one produced, as a reliable signal of mastery and skill. In the same manner, when LLM use is involved in the process of learning and work, one no longer can rely on the form of the output as a signal.

# Outcome vs. Process

When the output and outcome of work is no longer a costly signal, the process, more specifically the structure of the process that produced the outcome, can take its place. 

> When outcomes are cheap, costly (self-)signaling can appear in the *practices and process* used in generating the outcome. 

By using a structured way of working, and introducing constraints in the way writing is produced, we can make the process a more reliable self-signal of ability. I find the tools and techniques discussed in  [Active Learning](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Active_learning), [Cognitive Load](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cognitive_load), [The Generation Effect](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Generation_effect), [Forgetting as the Friend of Learning](https://journals.physiology.org/doi/full/10.1152/advan.00001.2019), etc. to be highly relevant to self-signaling. 

In Cognitive Load Theory, we observe that human working memory (which can be thought of as your live workbench where you hold and manipulate ideas in) is highly limited, enabling each person to manipulate between 3 to 7 "items" in mind at once. This acts as an information bottleneck to learning. In many learning scenarios, one can identify at least 3 distinct categories of load, the amount of information required to fit in working memory: 

1. **Extraneous Load**: Load that is inherently unrelated to the content of the information, but on how it is presented.
2. **Intrinsic Load**: The Load that is *inherently* part of the information itself.
3. **Germane Load**: Specific Load that plays a pedagogical role in helping the person, by introducing [Desirable Difficulties](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Desirable_difficulty).

LLM use, has the potential to greatly help in reducing (1) Extraneous Load. This can accelerate learning in many cases, and enabling active learning, by allowing for the rapid generation of interactive learning material. However, this can also mean that if not structured and constrained, they can also limit the Germane Load that naturally came with learning, and possible self-deception, or *Dishonest Self-Signaling*, though in many cases this is unintentional given the rapid change in signaling cost. If all we do during the process of learning, is prompting and further prompting LLMs, one can argue this to be a case of [Knowing the name (prompt for) something, rather than knowing that thing](https://fs.blog/richard-feynman-knowing-something/). I can see this as an actual concern.

Here are some of the ways I think we can reintroduce reliable self-signals, by shifting the focus to using the process of generation of writing as the signal, and some practices in LLM use, whilst learning.

1. **Self-Generation:** Writing the material oneself, forming each sentence, or formula, and typing/writing them ourselves, character by character, in your own words. Writing Descriptions, explanations, arguments, reasoning, and/or the story of that piece of information on in your own words.

There are many benefits to this, as it provides a lot of *Desirable Difficulty*, stimulating thought, and enabling [deep processing](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Levels_of_processing_model) of the information and concepts involved. In my view this is a very interesting way of making the process of understanding highly engaging, by directly interfacing with the concepts at hand. 

There are dangers and considerations whilst attempting Self-Generation. If we have spent a lot of time using an LLM, or reading from a specific source, how can know that the things we are writing are our *own* words, and not recollections of what we just read? To tackle this, I was inspired by [Spaced Learning](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Spaced_learning) (not to be confused with [Spaced Repetition](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Spaced_repetition)) and [Forgetting as the Friend of Learning](https://journals.physiology.org/doi/full/10.1152/advan.00001.2019). 

2. **Forgetting as a Friend of Signaling:** In order to provide ourselves with cleaner self-signals of competence, we can leverage forgetting. By either waiting a few days before attempting self-generation, or creating a buffer of interference with other tasks, we can provide ourselves with the opportunity to make signals of ability. If we can still express, using our own keystrokes and in our own words the information of interest, then perhaps we actually kind of get that thing!

I see this as the bread and butter of the [Zettelkasten](https://zettelkasten.de/overview/) method, though not the only relevant aspect. 

### But it's too slow!

Yes, by design. If one's only concerns is to produce a particular outcome, which arises in many situations, this is not in general required. If we are to however assume the process to be, to some extent, for our own [[Empowerment]], then of course: 

> Festina Lente

or 

> Slow is smooth, smooth is fast.


I *personally* think robbing ourselves of the opportunity to signal competence to ourselves, robs us of one the joys of living.
